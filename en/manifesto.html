Manifesto of the United Lives Nation

A movement for all living beings

I — The enchantment

Humanity has fallen under its own spell.

Every achievement — fire, cultivated land, the city, writing, the machine — increased its power to transform the world. And, along with that power, a belief grew: that the world exists for it. The more it achieved, the more it admired itself; the more it admired itself, the less it saw what it was destroying.

We call this enchantment species narcissism: the overvaluation of human beings by themselves, before all other living beings and the environment. It made us forget the simplest thing. We are one species among species, one life among lives. We are exceptional, but we are not exempt: what we do to the Earth and to other beings comes back to us.

We know this, and still we do not react. Not for lack of information. Recognizing the harm hurts, and the enchantment shields us from that pain. Apathy in the face of destruction is not ignorance: it is a defense. That is why no data, on its own, will make us change. We must change the place from which we look.

II — Overharm

Every life causes harm. Eating, sheltering, growing, defending oneself: to live is to take something from the world. This harm is inherent to life, and no law can abolish it.

But in nature nothing is wasted. What is taken returns to the cycle: what dies nourishes, what falls fertilizes. Species narcissism broke this cycle. It takes more than it needs, destroys what it does not use, hoards what it does not give back.

We call this overharm: harm beyond what is necessary. Excess, waste, destruction that neither serves life nor returns to it.

Overharm has many faces. The devastation of environments is the most visible. War is another. Inequality is another. They look like separate crises, but they grow from the same root: whoever learns to place themselves above life learns, sooner or later, to place themselves above other humans.

III — The law that protected the owner

Since the first codes, law has served to end vengeance, to say who owns what and to keep everyone in their place. It protected property, inheritance and hierarchy.

Other beings entered the law as things: cattle, land, timber, resources. Even today a river can be sold, a forest becomes stock, an animal can be discarded. The law was never neutral. It wrote the human enchantment down as rules.

That is why it is not enough to change habits, or to wait for each person to be converted. What the law protects must change.

IV — The fifth blow

Human narcissism has already suffered blows. We learned that the Earth is not the center of the universe. We learned that we are not a separate creation, but animals among animals. We learned that the self is not master in its own house. We are beginning to learn that we are not the only ones able to calculate and reason.

The fifth blow is still missing: human beings are not the only subjects of the law.

This blow does not lower us: it returns us to our place. Human specificity — speech, technique, a broad consciousness — stops being a title of ownership and becomes a duty of care. We are the ones who speak; that is why we also speak for those who cannot.

Every blow to narcissism meets resistance in proportion to the wound. This one will too. That is why it cannot be imposed: it must be recognized.

V — What we affirm

1. Interspeciesism. All living beings, of every species, and the environments that sustain them are under the protection of the law. Not because they resemble us, but because they live.

2. Measure. The harm inherent to life is accepted and reduced to a minimum. Overharm is not accepted.

3. Conflict without destruction. Conflict is part of life and will not disappear. We do not promise a world without conflict; we propose a world in which conflict need not destroy. It is resolved with the least possible harm, through listening and mediation.

4. There are no enemies and no criminals. There are conflicts, errors and consequences. Whoever errs bears, as fully as possible, the consequences of what they did, and those consequences exist to repair, not to punish.

5. Consciousness is responsibility. The broader a being's consciousness, the greater its responsibility, and never its power over another. Each one declares their own consciousness: whoever declares they see more answers for more.

6. Nothing is owned; everything is kept in care. The Earth belongs to no one. Stewardship belongs to whoever cares, for as long as they care.

7. An order of protection. First the environment that sustains life; then life as a whole, with the primacy of the collective; then each living being. And in each case, every life is preserved as far as possible.

8. Every voice is heard. Nothing is decided about a being without listening to it, and whoever cannot or will not speak is represented. Protest is never silenced: it is heard and answered, because it is knowledge.

9. To serve is not to rule. No one runs to serve the collective: they are called by their community, may decline and may be released at any time. They serve without privilege and with full transparency.

10. The necessary minimum. The law sets only the minimum for all to coexist. Everything else is decided case by case, with the least possible intervention.

VI — An order without weapons

Such a law needs no armies, no police and no penalties. Everything a being makes or uses is a tool; what matters is the use. Life in common is cared for by mediators called by the community itself and, only in the face of serious and imminent harm, by teams that remove the danger and hand the case over to mediation.

When someone must be contained, containment is dignified and reversible, and the collective receives it as a lament: something failed in us, who did not know how to bring that being into life in common. No consequence may take life, torture or silence.

Borders are forms of management, not walls of life. As long as the development of nations is driven by the war industry, peace will always be an interval. We do not ask for weapons to be laid down by decree. We are building an order in which they lose their meaning, as the weapons no one dares to use anymore have already lost theirs.

VII — The constituent revolution

The most necessary revolution of our time is a constituent one: to establish a law that guarantees the right to protection of the environment, of life and of every living being and, from it, the free development of all.

We do not want to seize power. We want to change what the law protects. It is a revolution without violence, because violence is precisely what it seeks to make useless.

That is why we propose a Constitution. It is not the whole of the movement: it is its instrument. It does not invent an order; it recognizes a law that already exists, the law of life, which follows its course, and extends it to human consciousness. That is why its core is irrevocable, and its text changes only to express that law better as knowledge grows. The practical norms adapt to each era, and each conflict is analyzed in its singularity.

VIII — With the world as it is

We do not wait for the world to change before we begin.

The United Lives Nation belongs to all beings, whether or not they have joined. To those who have not yet joined, we offer listening, mediation and example. To those who destroy, the refusal to cooperate with destruction. To those who refuse to sit down and mediate, an open door: we decide without them, but someone will also speak for them.

To those who have destroyed, we offer acknowledgment and repair, not revenge. To those who live from destruction, a conversion in which no one loses their livelihood. None of this is imposed by force. No one will be incorporated against their will, and the door remains open, to come in and to come back.

IX — The call

There is only one priority: to stop overharm. To stop the excessive and unnecessary destruction of environments, of life and of other living beings.

The revolution begins where life happens: in the building where neighbors do not know each other, on the street, in the neighborhood, in the village. It begins when a community gathers, adopts this proposal as a commitment and starts mediating its conflicts by it. Then come cities, regions, nations.

We do not ask anyone to surrender. We ask them to recognize.

We are one life among lives. It is time the law knew it.
