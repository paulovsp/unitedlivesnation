The instrument

Constitution of the United Lives Nation

The Constitution is not the whole of the movement: it is its legal instrument. It has two levels: a short core whose law is irrevocable, and the Statute of the AMC, which adapts to the knowledge of each era.

The reasons for this law are in the manifesto.

The seven principles

  1. 1

    Environment

    Every environment necessary for the free development of life is preserved.

  2. 2

    Order of primacy

    First the environment; then life as a whole, with the primacy of the collective; then each living being.

  3. 3

    Consciousness is responsibility

    The broader the consciousness, the greater the responsibility and the fewer the limits. Never more power.

  4. 4

    The measure

    Every interference with another being or the environment is weighed by the measure of responsibilities and limits.

  5. 5

    The AMC

    Concrete obligations come only from the bodies of Analysis of Mediations and Conflicts, subservient to the Constitution.

  6. 6

    A living Statute

    Responsibilities and limits adapt to the knowledge of each era.

  7. 7

    Least intervention

    Only the minimum for coexistence is set in advance; everything else is decided case by case.

Part I — The Constitution

Its law is irrevocable; the text is updated only to express it better.

Preamble

We, the living beings of the Earth, gathered in the United Lives Nation, recognize that no life exists apart from the environment that sustains it or from the other lives it lives alongside.

The Earth belongs to no one. The borders that divide it are forms of management, not borders of life.

We recognize no enemies and no criminals. We recognize conflicts, errors and consequences, and we mediate them.

We adopt this Constitution for the free maintenance of the environment, of life and of every living being. It belongs to all beings and prevails by example, never by force.

This Constitution does not invent an order: it extends to human consciousness the law of the universe, by which the environment and life follow their natural course. That is why its law is irrevocable, and its text changes only to express it better.

Principles

Art. 1 Every environment necessary for the free development of life is preserved.

Art. 2 The following are protected, in this order: the free maintenance of the environment; life as a whole, with the primacy of the collective over the individual; and the free development of each and every living being.

Art. 3 The broader a being's consciousness, the greater its responsibility toward everything outside it and the fewer the limits set for it in advance. Consciousness is self-declared: no one declares or contests another's. Responsibility never grants power over another being.

Art. 4 Every interference of one being with another or with the environment is mediated by the measure of responsibilities and limits, aiming at the free maintenance of the environment and of living beings.

Art. 5 The concrete obligations of beings are determined solely and exclusively by the bodies of Analysis of Mediations and Conflicts (AMC), which are subservient to this Constitution.

Art. 6 The AMC organizes itself and sets responsibilities and limits in its Statute, which it adapts, under constant review, to the knowledge of each era, according to the measure of Art. 3.

Art. 7 The AMC always acts with the least possible intervention, faithful to its own laws. Only the minimum necessary for the safety of the manifold coexistence on the planet is set in advance; everything else is decided by deep and singular analysis of each conflict.

Guarantees

Art. 8 In applying this Constitution, the preservation of each life of each living being is given the highest priority.

Art. 9 Nothing is owned: everything is kept in care. Stewardship belongs to whoever cares, for as long as they care.

Art. 10 Everything a being makes or uses is a tool. What is assessed is the use, never the object.

Art. 11 There are no crimes, no criminals and no predetermined penalties. There are errors and consequences, borne as fully as possible by whoever acted and aimed at repairing and returning the being to life in common.

Art. 12 No consequence may take life, torture, silence or expose a being as a spectacle. All containment is dignified and reversible.

Art. 13 Nothing is decided about a being without listening to it. Whoever cannot or will not speak is represented.

Art. 14 Protest is never silenced: it is heard, analyzed and answered.

Art. 15 The AMC serves; it does not govern. No one runs for it: those who serve in it are called, hold no more than one role, have no privilege and act with full transparency. Any act of the AMC contrary to this Constitution is void, and an independent body guards this Constitution against the AMC itself.

Art. 16 The foundation of the AMC is the community gathered, open to everyone's direct participation. Decisions are always made at the most local level able to resolve the conflict.

Universality, permanence and updating

Art. 17 This Constitution belongs to all beings and protects them all, whether or not they have joined the AMC. It grows through joining and example, and no one is incorporated by force.

Art. 18 The law this Constitution expresses is irrevocable, and this Constitution applies in full in every circumstance. There is no state of exception.

Art. 19 The text of this Constitution is updated with the knowledge of each era, only to express its law better. No update may reduce the protection of any being, give anyone power over another or contradict the principles.

Art. 20 Every update rests on the knowledge of its era and is examined by whoever guards this Constitution, who verifies that it respects Art. 19. It takes effect only after being confirmed by a majority of community meetings, in two consultations one year apart.

Part II — The Statute of the AMC

Revisable. It holds the norms and parameters of responsibilities and limits, adapted to the knowledge of each era and always subordinate to the Constitution.

Title I — Interpretation

Art. 1 The principles of the Constitution are parameters, not closed rules. They are fulfilled to the greatest extent possible in each case and guide both singular analysis and the general minimum.

Art. 2 Only when a conflict admits no other way out does the order of Art. 2 of the Constitution apply, from the whole to the being, weighing each one's measure of responsibility.

Art. 3 Every interference with another life is assessed by necessity, least harm, proportionality and the continuity of species and of the environment.

Art. 4 Natural processes among beings that cannot declare themselves are not conflicts under the Constitution.

Art. 5 Whatever is not limited and causes no harm is free.

Title II — Consciousness

Art. 6 The declaration of consciousness is made in a public register, from the age set by this Statute, and may change at any time, with effect only for the future.

Art. 7 Until this Statute sets others, there are three degrees of consciousness:

Declared degree Limits set in advance Responsibility
Basic Broad: this Statute defines in detail what the being may do, and it has a Trustee's support Repairs the harm it causes, with mitigated consequences
Intermediate Those of this Statute Fully answers for the harm it causes
Full Only the criteria of Title I Fully answers for the harm and for the risks it takes on

Sole paragraph. Whoever is able and does not declare is placed in the intermediate degree.

Art. 8 Consequences fall on whoever acted, according to the degree declared at the time of the act, and no later change of declaration erases them.

Art. 9 Children have full protection and remain in the basic degree until they can declare themselves. Beings that cannot declare themselves are protected by public criteria of the Council of Science and Knowledge; when in doubt, by the greatest protection.

Title III — Stewardship

Art. 10 Stewardship grants use and obliges the steward to return what is kept in living condition for other beings and for future generations. Whoever abandons or degrades what they keep loses its stewardship.

Art. 11 Whoever keeps more answers for more: stewardship beyond need generates a proportional contribution, which sustains the Nation and is applied publicly. What is necessary for life, such as housing, food and care, is never subject to contribution.

Art. 12 Indigenous peoples and traditional communities are the stewards of the territories they occupy.

Title IV — Mediation and consequences

Art. 13 Before any decision, the parties are heard and seek a solution together through mediation. Without agreement, a Mediation Chamber decides, in writing, with public reasoning and the right of appeal.

Art. 14 When a party refuses to sit down to mediation, the Chamber decides in its absence, with the least possible harm, and calls someone to speak for it, as the Trustees speak for those who cannot speak. The door to mediation remains open.

Art. 15 All harm generates consequences, defined by singular analysis. No limit of this Statute applies to acts prior to its publication.

Art. 16 Containment is the last resort. It lasts as long as there is risk to other beings, even for a whole life; it is reviewed periodically and carried out in a dignified, suitable and comfortable place.

Art. 17 Every containment is a lament of the collective, which failed to integrate that being into the environment. The community it came from takes part in analyzing that failure, and the AMC has the duty to correct it.

Art. 18 Those who suffered harm, and their own, are repaired and supported by the collective.

Art. 19 Similar cases are treated similarly, unless a reasoned difference applies.

Title V — Listening, protest and knowledge

Art. 20 The AMC receives every protest, listens to those who protest and answers publicly, with reasons and within a set time.

Art. 21 Protest is a production of knowledge. It guides the AMC and may open the review of any norm or decision.

Art. 22 A demand contrary to the Constitution is not granted: it receives a limit, never silence. The limit falls on the act that causes harm, never on expression, and those who protest may appeal to the Constitutional Court and to the Office of the Defender of Life.

Art. 23 Every decision, every analysis of collective failure and every answer to protest form part of the Common Memory, a public archive from which the AMC draws knowledge to revise this Statute, preserving the dignity of the beings involved.

Title VI — Life in common and calling

Art. 24 The foundation of the Nation is life in common. Each community, whether a building, street, neighborhood or village, meets regularly, and the community meeting is the first body of the AMC, open to everyone's direct participation.

Art. 25 Every role of collective management is filled by calling: the community calls whoever it recognizes as most effective for the collective. The person called may decline without any consequence, and the community may release them at any time.

Art. 26 Every calling is temporary and is not renewed immediately.

Art. 27 Calling happens in layers. Communities call, from among those who served well locally, those who will serve in the bioregion; bioregions call, in the same way, those who will serve the planet.

Art. 28 The same rules apply at every scale, from the building to the planet. Decisions are always made at the most local level able to resolve the conflict.

Title VII — Bodies of the AMC

Art. 29 In the bioregions and on the planet, the AMC is made up of separate, collegiate bodies: the Assembly, which approves this Statute at its scale; the Mediation Chambers, which mediate and decide cases; the Constitutional Court, which voids whatever contradicts the Constitution; the Council of Science and Knowledge, which gives opinions on the state of the environment and of life; and the Coordination, which carries out decisions. No one sits on two bodies at once.

Art. 30 Those who serve in the AMC receive enough to live on, according to the average income of the bioregion where they serve, with no additional benefit, and may have no interest in what they decide.

Art. 31 Coexistence is cared for exclusively by mediating agents of the shared space and by removal teams, called by the community where they act.

Art. 32 Removal teams act only in the face of serious and imminent harm. They remove the being from the situation, with only the containment tools strictly needed and the least possible harm, and hand it over at once to mediation. They do not investigate, judge or decide consequences. Every removal is recorded, made public and analyzed.

Art. 33 The Trustees of Life speak for beings that cannot declare themselves, for ecosystems and for future generations. They are called from among those who show care for them, have no interest in what they protect, and have voice and vote in the Assemblies.

Art. 34 The Office of the Defender of Life guards the Constitution against the AMC itself. It receives complaints from any being and may suspend any act of the AMC until the Court decides.

Title VIII — Joining and relations with the world

Art. 35 Joining the AMC is one's own decision and happens in stages: first people and communities, then cities and regions and, finally, nations, whose borders become management boundaries between bioregions.

Art. 36 On joining, a territory may open a public process of listening to and acknowledging the harms of its past, aimed at repair and reconciliation, never at punishment.

Art. 37 On joining, a territory's activities that do not fit the Constitution are converted to the stewardship of life, without anyone losing their livelihood and at the same pace as neighboring territories, with public verification.

Art. 38 On joining, no right already guaranteed is reduced. Until the Nation is complete, any member may leave by the same path it came in, and the door remains open to its return.

Art. 39 With those who have not joined, the Nation relates through listening, mediation and example. In the face of organized violence, it responds with non-cooperation, with a record in the Common Memory and with mediation always on offer.

Title IX — Revision of this Statute

Art. 40 Every norm of this Statute expires in ten years unless it is reviewed and confirmed.

Art. 41 This Statute is revised by the Assembly at each scale, with the opinion of the Council of Science and Knowledge, on the proposal of any body of the AMC, of the Office of the Defender of Life or of a protest. Every revision is public and may be rejected by a majority of the community meetings at its scale.

Art. 42 Any norm of this Statute that contradicts the Constitution is void, and the Court so declares.
